MEASURED / PUBLISHEDEvent times are from published reports: NOAA Service Assessment (2004) for 2003; ESA monitoring pages for Nov 2025 and Jan 2026; the NASA CCMC CME Scoreboard for Mar 2015; Kataoka et al. (2022) and Fang et al. (2022) for 2022; Knipp et al. (2018) for 1972. Flare light takes about 8.3 minutes (UK Department for Transport: 8.5); a CME (coronal mass ejection) usually takes about 1–3 days (DfT 2025); spacecraft at L1 give about 15–30 minutes' final warning (Royal Academy of Engineering 2013). In my own data, 48% of G3+ storms (1996–2026) came within 3 days of an M5+ flare, against about 20% by chance. ILLUSTRATIVEThe drawing: the cloud is shown moving at a steady speed and reaching L1 about 20 minutes before Earth; real clouds speed up or slow down. Times are UTC.
